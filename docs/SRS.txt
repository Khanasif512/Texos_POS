1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the complete functional and non-functional requirements
for a multi-tenant Point-of-Sale (POS), Inventory, Import/LC Management, and business-finance SaaS platform.
The first tenant and reference client is GK Interior, a Bangladeshi importer and retailer of aluminium, glass, and
home-decor items sourced from China. This document is written specifically to be consumed by an AI coding
agent (Claude Code) as the primary build specification. It defines entities, fields, business rules, calculations,
workflows, and acceptance criteria in enough detail that the system can be implemented with minimal additional
clarification.

1.2 Scope

The system to be built is a web-based, multi-tenant SaaS application named (working title) "ImportPOS" that
allows any company with an import  inventory  sales business model to:

  · Register as a company (tenant) using only an email address, and manage its own isolated workspace.
  · Manage suppliers, Letters of Credit (LC), purchase orders, and all import-related landed costs.
  · Automatically distribute LC-related costs (LC opening cost, freight, port cost, CNF cost, transport cost,

      unloading cost, delivery cost, customs duty, and other charges) across purchased products by weight or by
      piece quantity, to compute an accurate per-unit landed cost in BDT (Taka) from a foreign-currency
      (RMB/USD) purchase price.
  · Maintain inventory with product variations, multi-LC batch tracking, and branch-wise stock.
  · Record sales via cash and credit invoices, print delivery challans, and record money receipts.
  · Manage customers (CRM) including credit limits, opening balances, ledgers, and outstanding balances.
  · Track daily business expenses, including a dedicated LC-linked delivery-charge entry workflow.
  · Manage multiple branches and employees per branch with granular, role-based access control.
  · Generate operational and financial reports: purchase, sales, profit/loss, inventory, branch-wise inventory,
      salesman-wise sales, returns, delivery charges, top-selling items, and more.
  · Operate as a subscription-based SaaS product with plan management, billing, and self-service company
      onboarding.
Out of scope for v1.0: mobile native apps (a responsive web app is in scope), direct integration with Bangladesh
Bank LC systems or payment gateways (manual data entry is assumed), and automated customs/tax filing. These
may be considered in a future phase.

1.3 Intended Audience

  · Claude Code (or any AI/human engineering team) implementing the system from this document.
  · Texon's project lead (Ashraful) as the vendor and technical decision-maker.
  · GK Interior's management as the pilot client and primary source of business rules.
  · Future SaaS customers with similar import-to-retail business models.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

The product is a new, standalone, multi-tenant SaaS web application -- not an extension of an existing system.
Each subscribing company ("tenant") operates in a fully data-isolated workspace with its own branches,
employees, suppliers, LCs, products, customers, and financial records. A separate Super Admin (platform owner)
layer manages tenant onboarding, subscription plans, billing, and platform-wide monitoring.

2.2 Business Process Context -- GK Interior's Import-to-Sale Workflow

The functional design is driven by the following real business process, which the system must fully support
end-to-end:

  1. Supplier Selection: GK Interior identifies and fixes a supplier in China for aluminium, glass, or home-decor
      products.

  2. LC Opening: GK Interior opens a Letter of Credit through a Bangladeshi bank to guarantee payment to the
      supplier. The LC has a number, issuing bank, value, currency, and associated cost (LC opening cost/charge).

  3. Order Placement: A purchase order is placed against the supplier, referencing the LC, listing products and
      quantities and their agreed unit price in RMB or USD.

  4. Supplier Delivery & Shipping: The supplier manufactures/ships the goods. Freight, port cost, CNF cost, and
      transport cost are incurred to bring the shipment to Bangladesh.

  5. Arrival & Cost Finalization: When the shipment (against a given LC) arrives in Bangladesh, GK Interior
      records the actual RMB/USD-to-BDT exchange rate used, and finalizes all additional charges: LC opening
      cost, freight, port cost, CNF cost, transport cost, unloading cost, delivery cost, customs duty, and any other
      charges.

  6. Cost Distribution (Landed Cost Calculation): The total of these additional charges is distributed
      proportionally across every product unit in that LC shipment, using either weight-based or
      quantity(piece)-based distribution (selectable per LC/shipment), to produce a final per-unit landed cost in
      BDT.

  7. Inventory Entry: Each product (with its variations, e.g., 3 aluminium profile variations with different prices)
      is entered into inventory at its computed landed cost, tagged to its originating LC/batch.

  8. Sales: Products are sold for cash or on credit. Two invoice types exist (cash invoice, credit invoice), and a
      separate delivery challan is printed for the delivery person.

  9. Post-Sale Operations: Returns, invoice cancellations, credit notes, order cancellations, and money receipts
      against credit invoices are all tracked.

  10.Reporting & Closing: Daily cash closing, daily/monthly profit & loss, and a wide range of operational reports
      are generated for management.

2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints

  · The system will be built using the MERN-family stack with MySQL as the relational database
  · The system MUST be multi-tenant from day one; tenant data isolation is a hard security requirement, not a

      future enhancement.
  · All monetary values MUST be stored with explicit currency and, where foreign-currency purchases are

      involved, with the exchange rate used and the resulting BDT value -- never store only a converted number
      without retaining the source values.
  · All cost-distribution and landed-cost calculations MUST be reproducible/auditable: the system must be able
      to show exactly how a per-unit cost was derived from an LC's total charges.
  · The UI MUST be optimized for fast, low-click daily use at a sales counter (invoice creation) as well as
      detailed back-office data entry (LC, expenses).

2.6 Assumptions and Dependencies

  · Exchange rates (RMB/USD to BDT) are entered manually per LC/shipment by an authorized user; no live FX
      API is required for v1.0 (may be added later as an enhancement).

  · Bank LC issuance itself happens outside the system (at the bank); the system only records LC data and costs,
      it does not communicate with the bank.

  · Each tenant is a single legal company that may operate multiple physical branches under one subscription.
  · Users have basic literacy in English and/or Bengali; the UI should support both where feasible (see §6.4).

3. System Architecture Overview

3.1 Multi-Tenancy Model

Approach: Shared database, shared schema, tenant-scoped by company_id (a "pool" model). Every tenant-owned
table includes a company_id foreign key, and every query MUST be scoped by the authenticated user's
company_id at the application/service layer (never trust a client-supplied company_id). This is simpler to operate
and scale for a v1.0 SaaS than database-per-tenant, while still allowing a later migration path to schema-per-tenant
for large enterprise customers if needed.

  · A `companies` table represents each tenant.
  · A `branches` table belongs to a company (1 company : many branches).
  · Every operational table (products, LCs, invoices, expenses, customers, suppliers, etc.) carries both

      company_id and, where relevant, branch_id.
  · A global middleware attaches the requesting user's company_id (and permitted branch_ids) to every request

      context after JWT verification, and all Sequelize/Prisma queries apply this scope automatically (e.g., via a
      query middleware / scope helper) to prevent cross-tenant data leakage.

3.2 High-Level Component Diagram (Textual)

  · React SPA (Web Client) -- role-aware UI, calls REST API over HTTPS.
  · Express REST API -- organized by module (auth, tenants, lc, purchase, inventory, sales, crm, expense,

      finance, reports, admin).
  · Auth & RBAC Middleware -- verifies JWT, resolves company/branch scope and permissions before every

      controller.
  · MySQL Database -- single relational store for all tenant and platform data (see §5 Data Model).
  · Redis + Job Queue -- subscription checks, notifications, scheduled report generation, low-stock alerts.
  · Object Storage -- product images, LC documents, invoice/challan PDFs.
  · Super Admin Portal -- a separate protected area of the same app (or a distinct app) for platform management.

3.3 API Design Guidelines

  · RESTful, versioned endpoints under /api/v1/. Resource-oriented URLs (e.g., POST /api/v1/lc, GET
      /api/v1/lc/:id/cost-distribution).

  · Consistent JSON response envelope: { success, data, message, meta } for success; { success:false, error: {
      code, message, details } } for errors.

  · Pagination via ?page=&limit=; filtering via query params; sorting via ?sort=field:asc|desc.
  · All list endpoints for financial/report data MUST support date-range filters (from, to) and branch filters.
  · Idempotency: financial-mutation endpoints (invoice creation, LC cost finalization) should accept an

      idempotency key to avoid duplicate submissions from double-clicks or retries.
  · All monetary amounts transmitted as strings or fixed-point decimals (never raw floats) to avoid rounding

      errors; store as DECIMAL(15,2) in MySQL.

4. Functional Requirements

Each module below lists: Description, Key Fields/Entities, Business Rules, and Acceptance Criteria. Requirement
IDs are provided for traceability.

4.1 Module: SaaS Tenant & Subscription Management [TEN]

Allows a brand-new company to self-register, and allows the Super Admin to manage subscription plans, billing,
and tenant lifecycle.

Key Entities / Fields
  · Company: id, name, business_type, address, logo, primary_email, phone, currency (default BDT), created_at,
      status (trial/active/suspended/cancelled).
  · SubscriptionPlan: id, name (e.g., Starter/Growth/Enterprise), price, billing_cycle (monthly/yearly),
      max_branches, max_employees, feature_flags (JSON: LC module on/off, CRM on/off, advanced reports
      on/off, etc.).
  · Subscription: id, company_id, plan_id, status (trial/active/past_due/cancelled), trial_ends_at,
      current_period_start/end, auto_renew.
  · Invoice (Billing): id, company_id, subscription_id, amount, status (paid/unpaid/failed), issued_at, paid_at,
      payment_reference.

Business Rules
  · TEN-01: A new company MUST be created purely by providing an email address (plus company name) --
      the system sends a verification/OTP email, and upon verification creates the Company, a default Company
      Owner user, and a default 'Head Office' branch automatically.
  · TEN-02: Every new company starts on a configurable free trial (e.g., 14 days) on a default plan; the system
      MUST block access to paid-tier features once the trial expires until a plan is chosen and paid.
  · TEN-03: Feature access (e.g., number of branches, employees, whether advanced reporting is enabled)
      MUST be enforced server-side based on the company's active plan's feature_flags -- not just hidden in the
      UI.
  · TEN-04: Super Admin can manually override a company's plan, extend trials, suspend, or reactivate a tenant.
  · TEN-05: When a subscription lapses (payment fails and grace period expires), the company's data remains
      intact but write access is restricted to billing/read-only until payment resumes.

Acceptance Criteria
  · Given a valid email, a user can create a company workspace end-to-end (signup  verify  first login) in
      under 2 minutes without support intervention.
  · A company on the 'Starter' plan attempting to add a branch beyond its plan limit receives a clear upgrade
      prompt and the action is blocked server-side.

4.5 Module: Letter of Credit (LC) Management [LC] -- Core Module

                                                                                    Page 8 of 23
                                                                                                                                                  SRS -- GK Interior POS SaaS

This is the most business-critical module. It governs how import costs become per-unit inventory cost.

Key Entities / Fields
  · LC: id, company_id, lc_number (unique per company), issuing_bank, supplier_id, currency (RMB/USD),
      lc_value (foreign currency), exchange_rate_to_bdt, lc_opening_cost (BDT), status
      (draft/opened/goods_in_transit/received/cost_finalized/closed), opened_date, expected_arrival_date,
      actual_arrival_date, notes, attached_documents.
  · LCCostItem: id, lc_id, cost_type (enum: lc_opening_cost, freight, port_cost, cnf_cost, transport_cost,
      unloading_cost, delivery_cost, customs_duty, other), amount_bdt, description, entry_date, entered_by.
  · LCProductLine: id, lc_id, product_id/variation_id, quantity, unit_weight (if weight-based distribution used),
      unit_price_foreign_currency, line_value_foreign_currency.
  · LandedCostResult (derived/stored snapshot): lc_id, product_variation_id, base_cost_bdt (converted from
      FX), distributed_extra_cost_bdt, final_landed_unit_cost_bdt, calculation_basis (weight/quantity),
      computed_at.

Workflow / Business Rules
  · LC-01: Creating an LC requires: supplier, LC number, issuing bank, currency, LC value, and at least one
      product line (product/variation, quantity, unit price in foreign currency).
  · LC-02: The system MUST support recording ALL of the following cost types against an LC, matching the
      client's cost sheet: LC Opening Cost, Product Purchase Value, Freight, Port Cost, CNF Cost, Transport Cost,
      Unloading Cost, Delivery Cost, Staff Salary (if allocated to a shipment), Entertainment (if allocated),
      Customs Duty, and a generic 'Other Charges' category -- each as a separate LCCostItem row so every charge
      is individually auditable.
  · LC-03: Users MUST enter or update the exchange rate (RMBBDT or USDBDT) for the LC before cost
      finalization; the base product cost in BDT = quantity × unit_price_foreign_currency × exchange_rate.
  · LC-04: Cost Distribution Engine -- once all cost items are entered and the LC is marked 'ready to finalize',
      the system computes total_extra_cost_bdt = sum of all LCCostItem amounts EXCLUDING the base product
      purchase value. This total is distributed across every unit in the LC's product lines using the user-selected
      basis: (a) Weight basis: each unit's share = total_extra_cost_bdt × (unit_weight × quantity_of_that_line) /
      (sum of weight×quantity across all lines); or (b) Quantity/piece basis: each unit's share = total_extra_cost_bdt
      × quantity_of_that_line / (total quantity across all lines).
  · LC-05: final_landed_unit_cost_bdt (per SKU/variation) = base_cost_per_unit_bdt (from FX conversion) +
      distributed_extra_cost_per_unit_bdt. This value becomes the inventory cost basis when goods are received
      into stock.
  · LC-06: The system MUST retain the full calculation trail (all cost items, the basis used, and per-line results)
      so any landed cost can be explained/audited later -- never store only the final number.
  · LC-07: An LC can be edited (cost items added/corrected) even after initial receipt, but any correction MUST
      trigger a recalculation and create a new LandedCostResult snapshot with a timestamp and reason, without
      silently overwriting historical inventory valuations already sold.
  · LC-08: A single product/SKU MAY exist across multiple LCs (multiple batches) simultaneously; inventory
      MUST track stock by LC/batch internally (for accurate costing and profit calculation, using FIFO by default)
      while presenting a single, correctly aggregated total-stock view to the user.
  · LC-09: The Delivery Charge module (§4.10) MUST allow entering a delivery cost against a specific LC
      number, which also feeds into that LC's LCCostItem set for costing accuracy.

                                                                                                                                                  SRS -- GK Interior POS SaaS

  · LC-10: LC status progresses: Draft  Opened  Goods in Transit  Received  Cost Finalized  Closed.
      Only 'Received' or later LCs can have inventory posted from them.

Acceptance Criteria
  · Given an LC with 3 cost items and 2 product lines with different weights, selecting 'weight basis' produces
      per-unit landed costs whose extra-cost portion is proportional to each line's total weight, and the sum of all
      distributed extra cost across all units exactly equals the entered total extra cost (± rounding tolerance of the
      smallest currency unit).
  · Switching the distribution basis from weight to quantity for the same LC (before finalization) recalculates and
      shows both results for comparison.
  · A user can open the LC detail page and see, for any product, exactly which cost items and what proportion
      contributed to its final landed cost.

4.6 Module: Purchase Order Management [PO]

Key Entities / Fields
  · PurchaseOrder: id, company_id, branch_id, supplier_id, lc_id (nullable if non-LC purchase is allowed),
      order_date, expected_delivery_date, status (pending/partially_received/received/cancelled).
  · PurchaseOrderLine: id, po_id, product_variation_id, quantity, unit_price_foreign_currency or unit_price_bdt.

Business Rules
  · PO-01: A Purchase Order is normally created against an LC (for imported goods) but the system SHOULD
      also support non-LC local purchases (direct BDT purchase from a local supplier) for flexibility.
  · PO-02: Receiving a Purchase Order's goods (fully or partially) triggers Goods Receipt into inventory at the
      applicable unit cost (landed cost for LC-based POs, or entered cost for local POs).
  · PO-03: An order can be cancelled before receipt; a partially received order can only cancel the unreceived
      remainder.

Key Entities / Fields
  · Product: id, company_id, name, category, description, unit_of_measure (pcs/kg/set), image, status.

  · StockLedgerEntry: id, product_variation_id, branch_id, batch_id, transaction_type

Business Rules
  · INV-01: Each product supports multiple variations, each with its own price and stock; e.g., an aluminium
      product with 3 variations must show independent pricing and stock per variation.
  · INV-02: Inventory MUST track stock at the batch (LC/PO) level internally for costing (FIFO issue by default
      for cost-of-goods-sold calculation) but display a single accurate aggregated total per variation per branch to
      end users.

4.8 Module: Customer Management (CRM) [CRM]

Key Entities / Fields
  · Customer: id, company_id, name, phone, email, address, customer_type (retail/wholesale/dealer),
      credit_limit, opening_balance, current_balance (derived), status.
  · CustomerLedgerEntry: id, customer_id, transaction_type (invoice/payment/return/credit_note), amount,

Business Rules
  · CRM-01: Every customer has a configurable credit_limit; a credit invoice that would push the customer's

4.9 Module: Sales & Invoicing [SLS]

Key Entities / Fields
  · Invoice: id, company_id, branch_id, invoice_number (branch-scoped sequence), invoice_type (cash/credit),
      customer_id (nullable for walk-in cash sales), salesman_id, invoice_date, subtotal, discount, tax, total,
      paid_amount, due_amount, status (draft/confirmed/cancelled/returned).
  · InvoiceLine: id, invoice_id, product_variation_id, batch_id (for costing), quantity, unit_price, line_total,

Business Rules
  · SLS-01: Two invoice types are supported -- Cash Invoice (fully paid at point of sale) and Credit Invoice
      (partial/no payment, tracked against customer credit_limit and ledger).
  · SLS-02: Every confirmed invoice MUST automatically deduct stock (FIFO across available batches for that
      variation/branch) and create corresponding StockLedgerEntry rows.

  · SLS-03: Every confirmed invoice generates a printable Challan (delivery note) as a distinct document from

  · SLS-04: Payments against a credit invoice are recorded as MoneyReceipts, which update the invoice's
      paid_amount/due_amount and the customer's ledger balance.

  · SLS-05: Invoice Cancel: allowed only before delivery/payment is finalized (configurable window) and

  · SLS-09: Salesman-wise sales MUST be trackable via the salesman_id on each invoice for the Salesman-wise
      Sales Report.

  · SLS-10: Profit per invoice line = (unit_price - unit_cost_bdt) × quantity, using the snapshotted landed cost at

6. Non-Functional Requirements

6.1 Security

  · NFR-SEC-01: Strict tenant data isolation -- no query may ever return data across company_id boundaries;
      enforced via a mandatory server-side scoping layer, not client-supplied filters.

  · NFR-SEC-02: All traffic over HTTPS/TLS; passwords hashed with bcrypt/argon2; JWT secrets and DB
      credentials in environment variables / secrets manager, never in source control.

  · NFR-SEC-03: Role- and branch-based authorization enforced on every API endpoint, server-side.
  · NFR-SEC-04: Full audit trail for financial and access-control changes (see AUD-01).
  · NFR-SEC-05: Rate limiting and brute-force protection on authentication endpoints; account lockout after

      repeated failed logins.
  · NFR-SEC-06: Regular automated encrypted database backups with tested restore procedures; point-in-time

      recovery target of 24 hours data loss.
  · NFR-SEC-07: Input validation and parameterized queries throughout (ORM-based) to prevent SQL injection;

      output encoding to prevent XSS.

6.2 Performance & Scalability

  · NFR-PERF-01: Core POS actions (create invoice, look up stock) MUST respond in under 1 second under
      normal load for a single tenant with up to ~50,000 SKUs and ~500,000 transactions/year.

  · NFR-PERF-02: Report generation for date ranges up to 1 year MUST complete in under 5 seconds, using
      appropriate indexing and, if needed, pre-aggregated summary tables for heavy reports.

  · NFR-PERF-03: The architecture MUST allow horizontal scaling of the API layer and read-replica scaling of
      MySQL as tenant count grows.

6.3 Availability & Reliability

  · NFR-AVL-01: Target uptime of 99.5% for the production SaaS environment.
  · NFR-AVL-02: Financial mutations (invoice, LC cost finalization) MUST be wrapped in database transactions

8. Worked Example -- Landed Cost Calculation

This illustrates the calculation Claude Code must implement (LC-03 through LC-05), using illustrative figures
consistent with the client's notes.

Scenario
  · LC-2026-0091 opened for Supplier 'Foshan Nanhai Guangjun Aluminium'.

  · Two product lines: Line A -- Aluminium Profile Type-1, 2,000 pcs, unit weight 0.5 kg, unit price 8 RMB.
      Line B -- Aluminium Profile Type-2, 1,000 pcs, unit weight 0.8 kg, unit price 12 RMB.

  · Exchange rate: 1 RMB = 16.50 BDT (entered by user at cost-finalization time).

  · Additional cost items entered: LC Opening Cost 15,000 BDT; Freight 40,000 BDT; Port Cost 10,000 BDT;
      CNF Cost 8,000 BDT; Transport Cost 12,000 BDT; Unloading Cost 5,000 BDT; Customs Duty 60,000 BDT.
      Total extra cost = 150,000 BDT.

Step 1 -- Base cost per unit (from FX)
  · Line A base unit cost = 8 RMB × 16.50 = 132.00 BDT/pc.
  · Line B base unit cost = 12 RMB × 16.50 = 198.00 BDT/pc.

Step 2 -- Distribute extra cost (weight basis)
  · Total weight = (2,000 × 0.5) + (1,000 × 0.8) = 1,000 + 800 = 1,800 kg.

  · Line A share of extra cost = 150,000 × (1,000 / 1,800) = 83,333.33 BDT  per unit = 83,333.33 / 2,000 =
      41.67 BDT/pc.

  · Line B share of extra cost = 150,000 × (800 / 1,800) = 66,666.67 BDT  per unit = 66,666.67 / 1,000 =
      66.67 BDT/pc.

Step 3 -- Final landed unit cost
  · Line A final landed cost = 132.00 + 41.67 = 173.67 BDT/pc.
  · Line B final landed cost = 198.00 + 66.67 = 264.67 BDT/pc.

If 'quantity basis' were selected instead, the 150,000 BDT would be split by piece count (2,000 vs 1,000 of a 3,000
total) rather than by weight -- the system must support both and let the user choose per LC, as specified in LC-04.
